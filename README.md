# FLYTAPE

Two flies playing techno at [flytape.solo.engineer](https://flytape.solo.engineer).
The music is generated by spiking neural networks whose wiring starts as real
fly-neuron anatomy. There are no scripted notes, no sample playback, and no
step sequencer. Every sound begins as voltage in a simulated neuron.

## It starts with the dataset

Everything here exists because of one dataset: 70 reconstructed neurons of
*Drosophila melanogaster*, downloaded from
[NeuroMorpho.org](https://neuromorpho.org) in CNG SWC format. They come from
the electron-microscopy labs of Bock (brain) and Williams (ventral nerve
cord), 36 neurons for the first fly and 34 for the second — 27,069 traced
cable points in total.

These are not point clouds. Each neuron is a strict tree: exactly one root,
every other node has a single parent, and no edge crosses a neuron boundary
(we checked — 0 violations across 27,000 points). That tree structure is the
whole foundation. When a spike travels from node to parent node, it is
moving along a neurite that an actual fly grew.

The two files, `public/data/fly-neurons-wire.json` and
`public/data/fly-neurons-janelia.json`, are disjoint neuron sets — two flies
that share no anatomy. Whatever differences emerge between DJ FLYWIRE and
MC JANELIA come from their different neurons and their different upbringing,
never from different code.

## From cables to a brain

Each cable point becomes a leaky integrate-and-fire neuron: accumulate
voltage, fire above threshold, reset, refractory period. The cable edges
become synapses, both directions, ~18% of them inhibitory. Two structural
additions are grafted on, both declared rather than hidden:

- **Branch grafts** — each node also connects to its two nearest neighbours.
  The sampled cables alone are too sparse to recruit a whole network.
- **Descending lines** — real fly CNS routes brain-to-VNC signals through
  descending axons that cable reconstructions don't capture. Each sensory
  channel is wired to its own motor pool, so a beat has a path to survive.
  An earlier version wired these at random and the flies produced noise;
  the labeled lines are what made rhythm possible at all.

Motor pools get a +0.35 threshold elevation: background noise can never fire
them. Only coordinated input — which training then sharpens — crosses.

## Growing up: training

Training is reward-modulated STDP, one causal rule. Sensory channels receive
corpus onsets (a 32-step rhythm, 4 channels: kick, snare, hat, bass-or-lead).
The network's own motor bursts are scored after the fact by a critic standing
in for dopamine — hitting the beat earns reward, firing off-beat costs more
than hitting pays. Synapses with a recent causal pairing strengthen; late
pairings weaken. A teacher force-fires motor targets early on and decays to
zero; from there the network learns only from consequences.

The checkpoint that ships is chosen by a teacher-free generation probe, then
polished by 40 more consequence-only epochs. Last, calibration measures how
much of the network actually participates during playback and adjusts
cascade gain and background hum — hum is sub-threshold, so it widens the
excited pool without ever firing a node on its own.

Current numbers, from `public/data/train-report.json` (shipped unedited):

| | DJ FLYWIRE | MC JANELIA |
|---|---|---|
| Neurons | 14,400 | 12,669 |
| Generation F1, teacher off | 0.41 | 0.37 |
| Onset selectivity (rhythm) | 1.9x | 2.3x |
| Node utilization | 83% | 81% |

F1 of 0.41 is not a typo and not hidden: the networks still invent most of
their notes (divergence 0.74/0.77). What training reliably produced is
timing — motor bursts cluster on the beat, the pools stay quiet between
notes, and both brains pass every gate with the teacher off.

## The live set

At runtime the corpus is only sensory context. The audio clock steps each
brain four substeps per 16th at 96 BPM; motor bursts of three or more
simultaneous spikes become voices — FLYWIRE's pools drive kick, snare, hat
and bass; JANELIA's drive ghost percussion and lead lines an octave up.
Channels with a written score voice only bursts that land near the score;
everything else in the crate is free. When both motor populations
synchronize in the same bar, the filter opens — the drop is a measured
neural event with an eight-bar cooldown, not a timer.

The records are corpora: the DJs' original upbringing (straight 4/4
techno, syncopated breaks), Beethoven cut into IDM breaks, Satie, Für
Elise, and original compositions. The site shows everything the brains do —
per-node firing on the brain renders, per-fly utilization, spike counts and
drop decisions in the audit log — all from the same spikes that make the
sound.

## What is modeled

The reconstructions are real; several things built on top of them are not,
and are worth stating plainly:

- Real flies have ~140,000 neurons and 51.7 million synapses. This is 70
  neurons and ~96,000 modeled synapses per brain.
- Cable edges are genuine; inter-neuron synapses, branch grafts, and
  descending lines are modeled structure, documented in the code.
- Thresholds, weights, and hum are modeled parameters. The training report
  records whatever they produce.

## Running it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # http://localhost:3000
```

Retrain from scratch (deterministic, seeded, ~90 seconds for both brains):

```bash
npx tsx scripts/train-brains.ts
npx tsx scripts/brain-probe3.ts         # offline smoke probe
npx tsx scripts/participation-probe.ts  # utilization probe
```

After retraining, bump `DATA_VERSION` in `src/lib/neural-sim.ts` — data
bundles are served with immutable cache headers.
